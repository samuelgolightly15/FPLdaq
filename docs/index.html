Performance benchmarks

Performance benchmarks

Two reference points for a gameweek's scoring: what the whole market earned, and what a passive manager tracking it would have earned.

FPLdaq SWAPPI, the Selection-Weighted All Player Points Index, takes every point scored in the Premier League and weights it by how much of the average manager's squad was invested in that player. Weights are frozen at each deadline, because squads lock then, so it measures what the market was holding when it locked rather than what it bought afterwards. It counts all 577 players, so it is not a score any manager could achieve: no eleven, no captain, no bench. It does reflect aggregate market behaviour, and serves as a guide to what a less engaged or disengaged manager might score.

The FPLdaq Template is a more realistic demonstration of what a manager could achieve by simply following the crowd. It is the affordable fifteen that overlaps most with what the market owns, picked under the real rules: two goalkeepers, five defenders, five midfielders, three forwards, no more than three from any club, and within the average manager's own budget. It starts the eleven most-owned of those fifteen in a legal formation and captains the highest-owned outfielder. Nothing is chosen with hindsight, so it is a benchmark that could actually have been followed.

The gap between them is the cost of playing the game as it is really played: a budget you cannot exceed, a bench of four, and one armband.

      Bought and sold today

      Change over the last 24 hours in the money the average manager holds in each player. Price rises count as money in, as well as new owners.
